# Grounded visual directions

Audience: visitors choosing an overnight stay or planning an event in Greiz. The website must make both actions clear, then help people verify the hotel, rooms, event facilities, surroundings, and arrival details.

Seven materially different worlds were ranked from the audience's real travel and hosting context. They deliberately avoid the generic hotel-photo-and-card default and a travel-poster treatment.

1. **Vogtland route index** — A folded regional excursion map resonates with guests planning castle visits, walking, and cycling routes. It uses contour lines, route keys, and a restrained paper palette; its wayfinding grammar can connect the hotel's pages.
2. **Railway departure board** — A printed station timetable fits people coordinating arrival and business travel. It uses disciplined columns, time marks, and clear hierarchies. Risk: it can make the hotel feel transit-like.
3. **Greiz museum accession card** — Historic-city visitors often encounter object labels and archive records. It uses catalog typography, dates, and archival paper. Risk: it can make the website feel like a museum rather than an open hotel.
4. **Hotel guestbook and key register** — An earlier considered direction. Its ledger logic organizes check-in, rooms, and events, but its paper-first language was superseded by the user's later choice of the city-guide concept.
5. **Event invitation and programme** — Wedding and meeting planners recognize a programme that clarifies sequence and place. It uses folded stock, clear timing, and tactile typography. Risk: it can over-prioritize events over overnight stays.
6. **Breakfast table linen** — The hotel's documented breakfast buffet and terrace suggest a domestic hosting ritual. It uses woven marks, table-plan rules, and warm neutrals. Risk: it can imply dining services the source does not promise.
7. **Town-house wayfinding system — selected visual concept 3** — The user selected the third concept in the presented three-option board: “Stadt / town guide.” The central address and Upper Castle nearby support civic orientation, town maps, and clear route labels. It uses an evergreen map field, cream type, fine ivory linework, a restrained brass marker, and direct paths to rooms, events, Greiz, and contact. Risk: schematic map art can imply exact geography if not labeled clearly.

The user most recently selected the third visual concept, “Stadt / town guide.” This later instruction supersedes the earlier guestbook choice. Its wayfinding grammar is a visual system, not a claim about the building's age or landmark status. Actual business facts stay primary.
